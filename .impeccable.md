## Design Context

### Users
Young Indian adults (20s-30s) splitting expenses with friends, flatmates, and colleagues. They use the app in casual social contexts — after a Goa trip, during a chai run, settling rent. They want to quickly see what they owe and settle up, not do accounting. Mobile-first usage patterns; web as a companion dashboard.

### Brand Personality
**Friendly, smart, reliable** — like a sharp friend who always remembers who paid. The app should feel trustworthy with money (foundation), fast and frictionless to use (UX), and fun without being childish (personality). Indian context is native, not bolted on — INR formatting, UPI settlement, desi copy.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Warm and confident**: Orange `#E8651A` accent conveys energy and warmth. Deep navy `#1a1a2e` splash/headers add gravitas. Warm shadows (`rgba(0,0,0,0.03-0.08)`), never cold or harsh.
- **Modern Indian fintech**: Aspires to CRED/Jupiter's polish and premium feel, but more approachable and social. Not as dark/moody as CRED — lighter, friendlier surfaces with bold accents.
- **Better than Splitwise**: Same domain simplicity, but significantly more polished, modern, and visually confident. Splitwise feels dated — HisabKitab should feel like the next generation.
- **Anti-references**: Generic Bootstrap/Material look. Cluttered dashboards. Cold, corporate blue palettes. Childish illustrations or excessive emoji. Anything that looks like a bank app.
- **Theme**: Web is light with dark frosted header. Mobile supports dark/light toggle. Dark mode uses deep navy (`#1a1a2e`), not pure black.
- **Typography**: Outfit (display) for headings — geometric, modern. DM Sans (body) for readability. Monospace tabular nums for all currency values.

### Design Principles

1. **Trust through clarity** — Money is sensitive. Balances, amounts, and who-owes-whom must be instantly scannable. Use color (green=owed, red=owe), monospace currency, and clear hierarchy. Never ambiguous.

2. **Speed is the feature** — Every interaction should feel fast. Micro-animations (0.2-0.4s), optimistic UI, minimal taps to settle. If it takes more than 2 taps to do the common thing, redesign it.

3. **Warm, not sterile** — Orange accents, warm shadows, subtle background decorations. The app should feel like it was made by people, not a committee. Personality in copy, not in visual noise.

4. **Indian by default** — INR formatting (lakh/crore), UPI-ready flows, familiar patterns from GPay/PhonePe. Don't localize a Western app — build for India first.

5. **Premium but approachable** — CRED's polish with WhatsApp's familiarity. Dark frosted headers signal quality; light content areas keep it inviting. Never intimidating, never cheap.
